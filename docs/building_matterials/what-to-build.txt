Yes. Since the Business Projection and MVP are now aligned, the website should become the client-facing sales layer for DocuFlow UG. It should not look like an n8n course project. This needs special attention because the assignment specifically penalizes vague pricing.

The website should have transparent UGX pricing.

Our interviews support an entry point around UGX 150,000/month, while document volume varies dramatically. That suggests volume-based tiers.

I would propose three website packages, but distinguish the interview-informed launch offer from future scale tiers.

Starter

UGX 150,000/month

Best for smaller teams starting document automation.

Possible scope:

One automated document workflow
Defined monthly document allowance
AI-assisted extraction
Structured output
Basic monitoring
Standard support

Setup: UGX 500,000

CTA: Start With a Demo

Growth

We should not invent a final price without cost/volume validation. Before publishing, we should derive this from expected document allowances and support requirements. A reasonable website architecture is nevertheless:

Growth
For businesses processing larger recurring document volumes.

Includes Starter plus additional document capacity, additional workflow integration, enhanced reporting and priority support.

Professional

For higher-volume or more customized operations.

Could include multiple workflows, higher document capacity, advanced integrations, reporting and priority onboarding/support.

The important thing is that we should calculate Growth and Professional prices before putting numbers on the live site. The assignment wants transparent rates, so they cannot ultimately say “Contact us.”

Site-wide design direction

Visually, I would make DocuFlow feel like a professional African B2B technology company, not a generic AI startup.

The design should be clean, spacious and confident: strong typography, restrained brand palette, subtle document/workflow graphics, rounded but professional cards, light animation, and excellent mobile responsiveness.

Avoid robot imagery, glowing AI brains, excessive gradients, fake dashboards, stock photos of people pointing at holograms, and excessive technical terminology.

The visual identity should communicate:

Reliable. Local. Practical. Secure.
